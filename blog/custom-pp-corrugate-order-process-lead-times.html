Custom PP Corrugate Orders: From Spec to Delivery Timeline

Ppcorrugate Editorial • September 2026

Understanding Custom PP Corrugate Manufacturing

Off-the-shelf polypropylene corrugate serves many applications, but custom orders unlock the full potential of PP packaging—exact dimensions that eliminate wasted space, proprietary colors that reinforce brand identity, specialized flute profiles optimized for your load requirements, and integrated features like die-cut windows or ultrasonic-welded components. The custom order process requires more planning than buying stock sizes, but the operational benefits often justify the investment for medium to high-volume users.

Custom PP corrugate manufacturing begins with extrusion or co-extrusion of the corrugated sheet stock, followed by secondary operations like cutting, printing, folding, and assembly. Unlike injection-molded containers that require expensive hard tooling, corrugate customization relies on relatively affordable cutting dies, printing plates, and folding jigs. This makes custom PP corrugate accessible at lower volumes than custom injection-molded packaging.

Specification Development and Sampling

The first phase involves translating your requirements into manufacturing specifications. You'll need to define sheet dimensions, flute structure (typically 2-10mm), wall thickness, color (natural translucent, white, or custom Pantone-matched), and any special features like perforations, score lines, or corner reinforcements. Providing CAD drawings or physical mockups accelerates this phase, but experienced manufacturers can work from verbal descriptions and application photos.

Expect one to two weeks for initial sampling after specifications are finalized. Custom PP corrugate sheets from Elipacko can be sampled using existing extrusion tooling with custom cutting dies fabricated quickly for dimensional validation. Samples should be tested in your actual application—load testing with representative products, cycling through your handling equipment, and exposure to your environmental conditions (temperature, moisture, UV if outdoors).

Tooling Requirements and Costs

For flat sheets or simple boxes with straight cuts and standard folds, tooling costs are minimal—typically $500-$2,000 for steel rule dies that cut and score the corrugate. These dies last for hundreds of thousands of impressions, making them cost-effective even for moderate production runs. Complex shapes with intricate cutouts, tight tolerances, or specialized folding patterns may require progressive dies or custom folding fixtures that increase initial tooling investment to $3,000-$8,000.

If your design includes printing, expect additional setup costs for printing plates or screens. Digital printing has reduced setup costs for short runs, but for high-volume production, flexographic printing plates ($500-$1,500 per color) deliver better unit economics. Amortize tooling costs across your expected order volume: a $2,000 die spread across 50,000 sheets adds $0.04 per unit, negligible for most applications.

Production Lead Times and Minimum Orders

After sample approval and tooling fabrication, production lead times for custom PP corrugate typically range from 4-8 weeks, depending on order complexity and current factory capacity. This timeline includes extrusion of custom-color resin if required, sheet production, secondary operations, quality inspection, and packaging for shipment. Rush orders can sometimes be accommodated with 2-3 week turnarounds if standard resin colors are acceptable and no complex secondary operations are needed.

Minimum order quantities vary by product complexity and manufacturer capabilities. Simple flat sheets with standard flute profiles might have MOQs as low as 5,000-10,000 square feet, while complex fabricated boxes with multiple folds, printing, and assembly could require 2,000-5,000 finished units. Prototyping and Iteration

Plan for iteration cycles. First samples rarely achieve perfection—tolerances may need adjustment, fold lines might require repositioning, or assembly methods could need refinement based on your production team's feedback. Effective custom corrugate projects include 2-3 sampling rounds with progressively refined specifications. This iterative approach adds 2-4 weeks to the overall timeline but prevents costly mistakes in production tooling and first-article runs.

Document everything: maintain detailed specification sheets, photographs of approved samples, and written records of any design changes. When you reorder months or years later, this documentation ensures consistency. Request that your manufacturer maintain tooling and specification records in their system, and verify these details when placing repeat orders to avoid inadvertent changes.

Ordering and Logistics Planning

Factor customs clearance and freight transit into your timeline. PP corrugate imported from Southeast Asian manufacturers benefits from zero-percent antidumping duties but still requires 2-4 weeks for ocean freight plus customs processing. Air freight can reduce transit to 7-10 days for urgent orders but adds significant cost—viable for tooling samples or rush replenishment, less practical for bulk production runs.

Establish reorder points based on your consumption rate plus the full lead time cycle. If you use 10,000 units monthly and production plus shipping requires 10 weeks, maintain at least 25,000 units in safety stock to buffer against demand spikes or supply delays. Some manufacturers offer vendor-managed inventory programs where they monitor your usage and trigger production automatically—valuable for high-volume standardized custom products.

Working with Custom Corrugate Manufacturers

Choose manufacturers with experience in your industry. Food-grade applications require FDA-compliant resins and documented quality systems. Automotive or electronics packaging demands ESD-safe formulations and validated cleanliness standards. Outdoor applications need UV-stabilized resins.
