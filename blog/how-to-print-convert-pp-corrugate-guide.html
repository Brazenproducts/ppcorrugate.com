How to Print and Convert PP Corrugate for Branded Packaging

Ppcorrugate Editorial • November 2026

Why PP Corrugate Needs Different Handling Than Paper

Polypropylene corrugate behaves nothing like paper-based board when it comes to printing and converting. Its non-porous, low-energy surface resists conventional inks, and its flute structure responds differently to die-cutting and creasing. Teams moving from cardboard to reusable PP for branded totes, trays, or boxes often hit avoidable quality problems simply because the process was not adapted. This guide walks through the steps that produce clean, durable, print-ready converted parts.

The payoff is significant: well-converted PP corrugated boxes combine the durability of reusable plastic with crisp branding that survives repeated handling and wash cycles, something disposable board cannot match over a program's life.

Step 1: Surface Treatment for Ink Adhesion

Polypropylene has low surface energy, so ink and coatings bead up and flake without preparation. The standard fix is corona or flame treatment, which raises surface energy enough for inks to anchor. Treated sheet should be measured with dyne test pens to confirm it meets the target, typically well above the untreated baseline, before printing.

  • Corona treatment: common inline step for sheet and roll stock
  • Dyne verification: confirm surface energy before each run
  • Treatment aging: print promptly, since treatment decays over time

Skipping verification is the most common cause of field failures, where print looks fine leaving the plant but rubs off after a few handling cycles.

Step 2: Choosing a Printing Method

Several methods suit PP corrugate, each with trade-offs. Screen printing delivers thick, durable ink films ideal for bold logos and high-wear surfaces. Flexographic printing fits high-volume runs with consistent artwork. Digital printing enables short runs, variable data, and fast changeovers without plates. For reusable packaging that cycles hundreds of times, ink durability usually outranks resolution, which nudges many programs toward screen or robust UV-cured systems.

UV-curable inks are a strong match because they cure hard and resist abrasion and washing, matching the service demands of returnable packaging better than solvent systems that can remain softer.

Step 3: Die-Cutting and Converting

Converting PP corrugate into finished shapes relies on precise die-cutting. Because the material is tougher and more flexible than paperboard, dies must be sharp and set to the correct depth to shear cleanly without crushing flutes at the cut line. Rotary and flatbed die-cutting both work; the key is tooling specified for plastic corrugate rather than repurposed paper dies.

  • Use dies rated for polypropylene thickness and flute direction
  • Control cut depth to avoid ragged edges and flute collapse
  • Account for flute orientation, which affects fold cleanliness

For complex geometries and tight tolerances, cut-to-size sheet is the cleaner starting point. Sourcing custom PP corrugate sheets from Elipacko in the exact gauge and dimensions needed reduces trim waste and keeps converting lines running without constant re-setup.

Step 4: Scoring and Folding Without Cracking

PP corrugate folds best along scored or creased lines that run with the flutes. Cross-flute folds need proper scoring to avoid stress whitening or cracking at the hinge. A well-formed crease yields a clean, repeatable fold that survives repeated opening and closing, which matters for collapsible totes and returnable boxes that are assembled and knocked down many times.

Step 5: Finishing and Assembly

Finished parts may be joined by welding, riveting, stitching, or adhesive systems formulated for polypropylene. Standard paper-box glues will not hold on PP, so adhesive selection must match the substrate. Welded and riveted joints typically give the strongest, most durable assemblies for heavy-duty reusable packaging.

Putting the Process Together

Treat the surface, verify it, choose a durable ink system, cut with plastic-rated tooling, score for clean folds, and join with PP-compatible methods. Each step compensates for the properties that make polypropylene superior in service but unforgiving in conversion. Done correctly, the result is branded reusable packaging that looks sharp on day one and still performs after hundreds of trips, delivering the durability and print quality that justify moving off disposable board.
